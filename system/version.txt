  v1.46 - force_stun: full redesign around a one-shot timer, replacing
           the affect-expiry-loop approach entirely.
           WHY: Mark reported the stunned character could still move to
           another room, and combat never resumed. Root cause: the
           mechanism relied on update_pos() to force POS_STUNNED via an
           AFF_TRUCE check - but update_pos() is only ever invoked
           reactively, from specific combat-processing call sites
           (damage(), the violence loop, a few others). force_stun's
           entire purpose is to STOP combat, so once it fired, nothing
           was left to call update_pos() again - position silently
           stayed whatever it was before the stun (usually standing/
           fighting), and movement's own Position-8 requirement never
           saw anything to block. This was a structural mismatch, not
           a small bug - relying on a combat-tick side effect for a
           spell whose whole point is to halt combat.
           NEW DESIGN: spell_force_stun now sets both characters'
           position to POS_STUNNED directly at cast time (immediate,
           unconditional - no dependency on any later tick), and
           schedules exactly one add_timer(..., TIMER_DO_FUN, duration,
           force_stun_resume, 0) call. Confirmed by reading
           violence_update() directly before relying on it: its timer/
           affect sweep runs every pulse for every character in
           char_list, not just those currently fighting - so this
           timer is guaranteed to fire on schedule regardless of
           combat state, unlike update_pos().

  v1.39 - Fixed chance() and chance_attrib() - a real, wide-reaching
